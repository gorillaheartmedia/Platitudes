“The secret to finishing things might just be starting them like you mean it.”

We blame procrastination, motivation, or Mercury in retrograde for half-finished projects — but often, the real culprit is a sloppy beginning. We dive in with excitement, skip the boring prep, and then act surprised when everything later unravels like a mystery sweater. A good start isn’t glamorous — it’s lists, structure, and that unsexy word: clarity. The better you open a project — defining the “what,” “when,” and “how” — the easier it is to actually close it without emotional carnage. So today, look at your pile of “almosts.” Maybe they didn’t fail in the middle — maybe they never had a strong beginning. Prepare with intention, start with precision, and watch how much less heroic finishing has to be.
