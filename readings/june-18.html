June 18

“You don’t calm the storm by yelling at the waves.”

We all try it — arguing with reality, insisting it shape up. But the waves don’t care. They crash because crashing is what they do. The trick isn’t to win; it’s to stand there long enough to remember you’re waterproof. Peace doesn’t mean stillness. It means learning which turbulence isn’t personal. The sea foams, the sky shouts, and yet there you are — unremarkable, breathing, steadying yourself against the pull. That’s not defeat; that’s grace under seawater pressure. So today, when life starts thrashing, stop negotiating with the tide. Anchor your feet. Let the storm have its moment. It always runs out of breath before you do.
