“Gratitude: because apparently, nothing’s guaranteed — not even Wi-Fi.”

We keep waiting for life to be perfect before we appreciate it, but gratitude isn’t picky. It doesn’t need ideal conditions — it just needs you to notice. The moment you stop chasing what’s missing, what’s here suddenly looks kind of incredible (and occasionally hilarious). Gratitude isn’t a mood; it’s a muscle. The more you flex it, the less impressed your complaints start to sound. You realize most of what’s “wrong” is just unthanked stability doing its job quietly in the background. So today, take a second to appreciate something absurdly ordinary — a good stretch, a clean spoon, someone who texted back. Gratitude doesn’t change the world, but it sure makes it easier to live in.
