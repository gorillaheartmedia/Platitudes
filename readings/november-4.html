“Even when the lights go out, the stars still shine.”

You go to the refrigerator seeking a late night snack only to be suprised that the light doesn't turn on when you open the door. Suddenly, the kitchen lights flicker, and then brown out, almost like a scripted Hollywood movie scene. It’s easy to feel alone when everything familiar flickers out — when plans fail, connections dim, or the world feels too heavy to hold. But darkness isn’t emptiness; it’s just a different kind of visibility. When the power cuts out, the stars finally get their metaphorical and literal "time to shine." There’s something out there — call it nature, chance, grace, or just good timing — that keeps watch even when you can’t. It’s in the friend who texts at the right moment, the stranger who holds the door, the absurd coincidence that reminds you life still has a pulse. So tonight, when things feel overwheleming, don’t rush to fix the dark. Sit in it. Look up. The universe has its own emergency lighting — it just takes a moment for your eyes to adjust. There might be something out there keeping the lights on for you.
