“Doing nothing is harder than it looks — but it’s worth the practice.”

We’re trained to chase productivity like it’s a moral virtue. Sit still too long, and the guilt creeps in — maybe you should be folding something, improving something, becoming something. But not every moment needs to earn its keep. Doing nothing isn’t laziness; it’s recovery. It’s what happens when you stop measuring your worth by output and remember that being alive is, frankly, enough work already. So today, stretch out, stare at nothing in particular, and let the world spin without your supervision. The dishes can wait. The laundry can fend for itself. You’re busy performing a sacred act of existential maintenance.
