December 18

“Every family has its own holiday physics.”

Somewhere between the burnt cookies, the off-key caroling, and that one relative who still wraps gifts in newspaper, you realize — perfection was never part of the plan. The holidays run on quirks. Without them, they’d feel suspiciously efficient. Every tradition starts as a mistake someone liked enough to repeat. The mismatched ornaments, the weird inside jokes, the annual debate over who really forgot the cranberry sauce — that’s the good stuff. That’s the texture of belonging. So today, lean into the beautiful mess. Laugh at the chaos, honor the oddities, and stop pretending any of this needs to make sense. The holidays aren’t meant to be flawless — they’re meant to be ours.
