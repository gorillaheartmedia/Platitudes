December 14

“Illuminating the neighborhood, one tangled wire at a time.”

There’s nothing quite like the annual outdoor light project — the ladder wobble, the frozen fingers, the string that suddenly stops working halfway across the roof. It’s a test of patience disguised as holiday spirit. But somewhere between the swearing and the extension cords, something shifts. The lights come on — unevenly, beautifully — and the whole street softens a little. You remember why people do this in the first place: not to impress, but to share brightness when the days are short. Stringing lights isn’t just decoration; it’s a declaration. A way of saying, “We made it this far, and we still believe in a little glow against the dark.” So today, plug in your masterpiece — gaps, flickers, and all. The beauty isn’t in how perfect it looks, but in the fact that you bothered to make the night shine.
